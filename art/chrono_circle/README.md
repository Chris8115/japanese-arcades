# Chrono Circle cabinet

Chrono Circle is the eighth cabinet in the **Japanese Arcades** creative tab.
It shares the other cabinets' three-part placement and native Minecraft
music-disc playback. The model follows the supplied cabinet photographs,
including the broad clock-like control face, reclined circular screen, character
hood, white enclosure, and projecting low floor platform.

Placement occupies **three vertical blocks**. The physical envelope is about
**1.30 blocks wide**, **1.89 blocks deep** including the platform, and just under
**three blocks tall**. Leave room at the sides for the clock enclosure and an
open aisle for the broad platform in front. Proportions
are interpreted from the photographs rather than manufacturer measurements.

The display, buttons, and other controls are decorative. Minecraft music discs
provide the playable audio; the cabinet does not run the Chrono Circle rhythm
game. The base emits light level 4 and both upper sections emit level 13.

## Use in Minecraft

Launch `dev.cmd Client` or `dev.cmd PackClient`, then select **Chrono Circle
Arcade Cabinet** from **Japanese Arcades**, or run:

```text
/give @s japanesearcades:chrono_circle
```

Place it on a solid floor with three clear vertical blocks. Its front faces the
player. Breaking any section removes all three; survival drops one cabinet item.

Right-click any section with a music disc to insert it and start playback.
Right-click again to eject it. Each cabinet stores one disc and saves its playback
progress. Modded songs with a valid jukebox-playable component use the same native
player. Breaking the cabinet stops the song and returns its disc once, including
in creative mode. Adjacent cabinets retain independent discs and playback. The
base provides jukebox redstone/comparator output and a one-slot container.

The disc ejects 1.15 blocks forward of the base center, above the low platform,
with its complete item bounding box clear of the cabinet. The platform has its own shallow
collision shape, leaving the space above it open. A separate shallow ledge at
the top of the base covers the reclined clock backing where it crosses the
section boundary. Restart the development client after Java changes; F3+T
reloads only models and textures.

| [display-artwork-source.png](display-artwork-source.png) | Original purple witch attract-screen illustration |
| [header-artwork-source.png](header-artwork-source.png) | Original ensemble poster for the upper character booth |

Twelve separate raised chrome-and-white buttons surround the recessed circular
display. A printed white clock face carries rainbow markings and a narrow
physical light groove. The deep upper booth combines a pale character poster,
four black speaker boxes, an angular dark canopy, orange perimeter lamps, and
a transparent acrylic sign with outlined white logo ink. The lower cabinet has opposing slopes,
white service doors, black ventilation, a small card reader, and a second
wordmark at calf level. The broad floor platform has clipped corners, clock
graphics, a luminous rainbow clock ring, and a thin orange perimeter rim.

The two source illustrations were created in **built-in image generation mode**.
Their original pixels remain unchanged in the saved PNGs. Runtime textures use
power-of-two padding, and the circular display is shaped by geometry and UVs.
Exact prompts and provenance are recorded in
[imagegen-prompts.txt](imagegen-prompts.txt). Material, clock, hardware, and
branding atlases are generated by `scripts/chrono_circle_textures.py`.

Regenerate with Python and Pillow, then render with Blender:

```powershell
python scripts/make_chrono_circle_model.py
blender --background --python scripts/render_chrono_circle_model.py
```

The generator uses `scripts/chrono_circle_shell_geometry.py`,
`scripts/chrono_circle_upper_geometry.py`, and
`scripts/chrono_circle_mesh_geometry.py`. A shared surface pass removes hidden
faces and clips overlapping opaque cuboid surfaces. The clock's circular layers
use 96 shared radial segments; each raised button follows four segments across
its short arc. This replaces tiny scanline cuboids and buried ring walls while
retaining the original radii, layer depths, twelve-button arrangement, 22.5-degree
inclination, and projected artwork. Static NeoForge OBJ models supply native
lighting and full-bright materials without a custom renderer.

The optimized export contains **7,008 world faces** across three sections:
**2,390 base**, **3,105 console**, and **1,513 marquee**. The item has **6,786
faces**. This reduces the former 14,920-face world/item models by **53.0%** and
**54.5%**, respectively. The editable source contains 6,834 faces before section
clipping and conservative coplanar merges. Its five shared runtime textures and
two original illustrations remain byte-for-byte unchanged. The outer cabinet
bounds, hood, lower housing, floor platform, and native placement stay unchanged.

The renderer reads the exported game geometry and texture atlases, produces
`chrono_circle-{front,back,side,controls,screen,marquee}.png`, and saves the packed
Blender scene. Append `-- --front-only` for a front preview or `-- --detail-only`
for controls, screen, and marquee views. Add `--quick` after `--` for 12-sample
previews. Studio lighting differs from Minecraft.

The generator is the canonical model source. Save manual Blockbench edits
separately or transfer them into the generator before regeneration replaces the
exported model files.

## Runtime implementation

The block/item ID is `japanesearcades:chrono_circle`. Registration begins in
`src/main/java/local/japanesearcades/JapaneseArcades.java`.
`ChronoCircleCabinetBlock.java` defines section bounds, compound body/platform
collision, and disc-ejection position. It inherits placement and interaction
from `NostalgiaCabinetBlock.java`. All cabinets share
`NostalgiaJukeboxBlockEntity.java` and Minecraft's native `JukeboxSongPlayer`,
retaining the existing saved-data and block-entity IDs.
